A design system that can fail a build

A design language written on boards is read once and then drifts. This one is a package: one mint, three springs, seven beats, and a set of rules about what an act in each part of an app is allowed to be. Those rules are lint, not advice — "groceries: items only, never a person" is a sentence on a board and an error against an act definition.

Say something

the card follows the sentence

Type as if you were speaking. The card lands, fills in the order you said things, holds, and commits on silence — drawn in the language rather than in whatever the renderer felt like. Switch the domain and watch the colour follow the meaning, never the theme.

Break the language

board 12, as lint

Edit the act below. It is composed against the closed capability registry — a model cannot lower blast radius — and then checked against the domain it claims. Give a grocery an assignee, or let an allowance settle on silence, and the check fails before anything is drawn.

The material

board 13

Fixed domain colours, emitted from one place. A member's accent pack never repaints them: resolveColours() takes the accent so that it can visibly not use it.

The budget

board 10

"Miss these and it stops feeling like a reflex." Measured on the clock, so a regression that adds 90 ms between the words ending and the card landing fails a test instead of being noticed in six months by someone who says the app feels heavier than it used to.
